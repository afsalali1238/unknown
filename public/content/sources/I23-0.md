> House-curated overview of Jack Dorsey's 2025–2026 "intelligence-native company" narrative around Block and accompanying commentary/critiques.
> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with the cited authors.

In 2025–26, Jack Dorsey framed Block as becoming an "intelligence-native company" — reorganizing the fintech group around autonomous AI agents rather than a traditional corporate hierarchy. Public reporting (Forbes, TechBuzz, and Dorsey's own posts) described plans to replace much of the traditional manager/org-chart structure with agents that coordinate work, with a much smaller human headcount — reports referenced thousands of roles at stake and a target operating model where humans set goals and exceptions while agents execute operational workflows (support, review, payments operations, software development, fraud operations).

The narrative sits at the extreme end of a real shift visible across the technology industry: coding agents that handle end-to-end feature development, LLM-driven customer support resolution, and AI co-workers deployed into internal workflows. Dorsey's bet, and the parallel claims from other founder-CEOs, is that the fundamental unit of production is changing: from teams of humans using software, to autonomous agents owned and configured by a small core of people, with human labor shifting toward specification, judgment, oversight, and the exceptions.

Skeptical analyses made several points. **Storytelling vs. substance:** reorganizations announced with maximum dramatic framing often work as investor narrative and recruiting signals; "AI-native org charts" in practice mostly re-layer human teams with tooling. **The hard parts remain human:** regulatory responsibility (a financial firm cannot outsource compliance liability to an agent), ambiguous decisions, cross-stakeholder coordination, and the fact that agents' reliability on long-horizon tasks is still poor. **Job-cut framing may exaggerate the AI causal chain:** many 2024–2026 tech layoffs reflected post-ZIRP cost discipline and over-hiring correction, with AI used as justifying language. **Incentive skepticism:** announcements that the future needs fewer workers are made by executives for whom worker leverage and wages are costs; they should be read with the same discount as any efficiency narrative.

Even granting the critique, the substantive direction is real at the margin: companies are benchmarking headcount against AI-augmented workflows, high-leverage operators managing fleets of agents is a genuine new job shape, and fintech (rule-heavy, text-heavy, repetitive) is among the sectors where automation is most immediately deployable. The open questions that matter for anyone planning a career or business: how much of the org chart actually gets replaced versus compressed over the next five years, how the surplus is distributed (price cuts, margins, wages of the surviving workers), and whether the "agent company" looks more like the industrial transformations that genuinely reorganized firms, or the "flat company" and "holacracy" waves that reverted once scale revealed the old functions (management, accountability, trust) as irreducible. The responsible posture: treat "intelligence-native" as a working hypothesis about where leverage moves, design skills for directing and verifying agents rather than competing with them, and discount predictions made by parties who benefit from the prediction.
