Mary Meeker (born September 1959) is an American venture capitalist and former Wall Street securities analyst, known as the founder and general partner of the San Francisco firm BOND (Bond Capital). She previously served as a partner at Kleiner Perkins, and before that as a star technology analyst at Morgan Stanley. She has been called the "Queen of the Internet" for her research on internet and technology companies, and has appeared repeatedly on the Midas List and Forbes' list of the World's 100 Most Powerful Women.

Meeker joined Merrill Lynch as a stockbroker in 1982, covered technology at Salomon Brothers after business school, and moved to Morgan Stanley in 1991 to specialize in personal computers and consumer software. In August 1995, Morgan Stanley led Netscape's IPO; the following February, Meeker and Chris DePuy published "The Internet Report," a landmark industry survey that became known as "the bible" for investors in the dot-com boom. It was shared widely on the web and also published as a book.

Over the following years Morgan Stanley published similar landmark reports led by Meeker on online advertising, e-commerce, the evolution of search, the Internet in China, and the mobile Internet. Her rapid-fire annual Internet Trends overviews at the Web 2.0 Summit in San Francisco became an institution: dense, data-heavy decks that assembled thousands of data points into a picture of where technology adoption was heading.

When the dot-com bubble burst in 2000–2002, Meeker was among the star analysts questioned in fraud investigations, though she was never charged with wrongdoing; Morgan Stanley and nine other firms entered the global analyst research settlement. In 2004 she was the research analyst on Morgan Stanley's lead-managed Google IPO. Fortune's Andrew Serwer described her as "absolutely first rate when it comes to spotting big-picture trends before they come into focus," gathering "massive amounts of data" into reports "stuffed with a million jumping-off points."

Meeker joined Kleiner Perkins in 2010 and later founded BOND in 2018. Her 2019 Internet Trends report — her 34th — and the BOND reports that followed continued to anchor the annual conversation about mobile, commerce, and the shift of the internet's center of gravity toward Asia.
