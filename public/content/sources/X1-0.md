> House-curated overview of perceptual processing.
> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with the cited authors.

The reality we experience is not a photograph of the outside world but a reconstruction — a controlled hallucination generated by the brain and constrained by sensory input. Neuroscience and philosophy of perception describe several strata between the world and experience.

**Stratum 0: the physical world.** Photons, pressure waves, molecules, electromagnetic fields — energy patterns carrying far more and far less information than we use; no color, no sound, no smell as qualities, just structure.

**Stratum 1: transduction.** Sense organs encode fragments of that structure into neural signals — the retina already compresses 130 million photoreceptors into ~1 million optic nerve fibers, pre-processing edges and contrast before anything leaves the eye.

**Stratum 2: feature detection.** Visual cortex assembles edges into orientations, motion, shapes; auditory cortex parses frequency and timing; areas specialize for faces, places, bodies. Classic Hubel–Wiesel recordings showed single cells responding to lines at specific angles.

**Stratum 3: predictive construction.** The dominant modern framework — predictive processing (Helmholtz's "unconscious inference," updated by Friston, Clark, Hohwy) — holds that perception is a top-down prediction machine: the brain constantly generates a model of the world, projects it, and updates it from prediction error. What you see is the best hypothesis the brain has, not the raw signal. This explains optical illusions (prior assumptions overruling data), multisensory illusions (the rubber hand, ventriloquism), and why ambiguous images (Necker cube) flip between interpretations without the input changing.

**Stratum 4: attention and salience.** The spotlight of attention selects fragments for conscious processing; inattentional blindness (Simons & Chabris's gorilla) shows most of the world is never seen at all.

**Stratum 5: cognitive/cultural overlay.** Perception is penetrated by expectation, language, and context — coins look larger to poor children, hills steeper to those encumbered; experts "see" chess positions and medical patterns novices cannot; cultural categories shape color perception and facial recognition.

**Stratum 6: the experienced world.** A stable, bounded, meaning-laden environment — with a sense of self at its center — that exists as a model.

The practical implications are large. First, most disagreement is not dishonesty: different models generate different realities from similar inputs, and people are literally perceiving different worlds. Second, beliefs and observations are not separable — we see what we expect (perceptual set), which is why exposure to diverse inputs and deliberate prediction-checking (forecasting, tests, red teams) is how the model updates. Third, the layer you can train is the model: reading, expertise, and frameworks literally change what you perceive, which is the cognitive justification for mental models — they are perception upgrades. Fourth, humility about "obvious" reality follows from knowing the construction process; the gap between the model and the world is where surprise — and learning — enters.
