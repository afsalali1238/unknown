> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with the author.

Naval Ravikant's "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" argues that wealth is a learnable skill, and that the core scarcity in modern economies is not labor, capital, or information — all abundant — but **judgment**: the ability to make good long-horizon decisions under uncertainty.

The core chain of ideas:

- **Wealth is not money or status.** Wealth is assets that earn while you sleep — businesses, equity, capital, software, media. Money is how we transfer time and wealth; status is your rank in the hierarchy. Seeking wealth and seeking status are different games; status is zero-sum, wealth is positive-sum.
- **You will not get rich by renting your time.** Even well-paid labor keeps income linear and requires your presence. Wealth comes from owning equity — a piece of a product, business, or capital that can scale without you. "You're not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity — a piece of a business — to gain your financial freedom."
- **Leverage is the engine.** Three forms of leverage exist: labor (other people working for you — oldest, hardest, requires permission), capital (money to scale — the twentieth-century form), and **products with no marginal cost of replication** — code and media (the new form, permissionless, "the most important one for our generation"). Software and content work while you sleep; they are the leverage available to anyone with a computer and no one's permission.
- **Build and use specific knowledge.** Specific knowledge is the knowledge that cannot be trained for or easily copied — found where your genuine curiosity and play meet the edge of what society can do. It looks more like a natural talent or obsession than a credential. If society can train someone else to do it, competition drives the wage down.
- **Combine specific knowledge with accountability and leverage.** Take risks under your own name (accountability is a force multiplier — people bet on named individuals); then apply code, capital, and media to scale the output.
- **Judgment compounds.** "Judgment is the scarcest resource" — because at scale, a good decision made for you by a leader is worth billions while execution can be delegated. Judgment is built by reading deeply (especially the foundational books of math, science, philosophy), by understanding mental models, by thinking clearly and for long uninterrupted stretches, and by avoiding people and environments that corrupt your thinking.
- **Patience and position.** Most of the returns come from a few long bets: "Play long-term games with long-term people. All returns in life, whether in wealth, relationships, or knowledge, come from compound interest." Choose industries and partners where you can play for decades.
- **The meta-advice:** learn to sell or build; ideally both. Become the person who can do one of those at an elite level and you can command equity; do both and you'll run things. Then escape the status game, optimize for freedom and peace, and remember that wealth buys time and autonomy — not consumption.

The Naval frame that travels beyond money: in a world of abundant capital and free information, the individual with permissionless leverage (code/media), specific knowledge, clear judgment, and a long time horizon captures compounding returns; everyone else competes in crowded, linear, permissioned markets.
