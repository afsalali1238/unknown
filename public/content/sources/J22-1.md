Charlie Munger's *The Psychology of Human Misjudgment* catalogues the recurring cognitive biases that warp judgment — his answer to why smart people do foolish things, and the "psychology" part of his latticework of mental models. Originally a talk (Harvard School, 1995), it became the psychological centerpiece of *Poor Charlie's Almanack*.

Key tendencies from the list (Munger names ~25):

- **Reward/recognition superpower** — incentives dominate; "show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome." Never ask a barber whether you need a haircut.
- **Liking/loving** and **disliking/hating tendencies** — we ignore faults of loved people and virtues of disliked ones; distortion compounds.
- **Doubt-avoidance** and **inconsistency-avoidance** — the brain rushes to remove doubt and then fights to keep decisions/identities consistent (commitment escalation).
- **Reciprocation** — gifts and concessions trigger repayment; used in sales and diplomacy.
- **Social proof** — uncertainty triggers imitation; Salomon and cults, crowds and bank runs.
- **Authority- and stress-influence** — obedience to authority (Milgram) rises under stress and confusion.
- **Deprival super-reaction (loss aversion)** — losses hurt ~2x equivalent gains; taking something away triggers irrational defense.
- **Contrast-caused distortion** — sequential comparisons warp perception (the real estate decoy).
- **Bias-from-commitment/consistency**, **Kantian fairness**, **envy/jealousy**, **availability**, **use-it-or-lose-it** (skills and mental models), and **lollapalooza** — Munger's term for many biases aligned in one direction, producing extreme outcomes (cults, bubbles, great products).

The method Munger prescribes: learn biases as a checklist; inspect decisions for which ones are firing; organize experience around them so they fire automatically; and remember the strongest warning is about *interactions* — single biases nudge, lollapaloozas convert into fraud, manias, and conversions. As a mental-models text it complements Kahneman's formalization with business-law case history.
