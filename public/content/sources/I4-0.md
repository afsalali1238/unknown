Founders Fund is an American venture capital fund founded in 2005 and based in San Francisco. The firm was organized by Peter Thiel, Ken Howery, and Luke Nosek — three early members of the PayPal team — and raised its first fund of $50 million from individual entrepreneurs and angel investors in January of that year. Sean Parker, co-founder of Napster and the first president of Facebook, joined as a partner in 2006.

The firm was the first institutional investor in SpaceX and Palantir Technologies, and an early investor in Facebook. Its portfolio has included Airbnb, Anduril, DeepMind, Stripe, Spotify, Neuralink, Rippling, Ramp, Polymarket, and Nubank. Its partners have historically been founders, early employees, and investors at companies including PayPal, Palantir, Anduril, and SpaceX.

The fund grew quickly: a $220 million fund in 2007, $250 million in 2010, $625 million in 2011, a $1 billion suite of funds in 2014, and a $1.3 billion fund in 2016. As of 2025 it reported roughly $17 billion in assets under management.

Founders Fund is closely associated with the worldview expressed in its 2011 manifesto, "What Happened to the Future," written by Thiel with Garry Kasparov? No — credited to Thiel and the fund's partners. The manifesto argued that the most valuable companies were founded on a secret — a belief about the world that few others shared — and contrasted "indefinite" pessimism with the "definite optimism" required to build new technologies. It helped define a generation of founder-led, contrarian technology investing, and made the firm a standard-bearer for the politics and philosophy of the PayPal diaspora in Silicon Valley.

Notable partners over the years have included Brian Singerman, Keith Rabois, Cyan Banister, Kevin Hartz, Sean Parker, and Bruce Gibney, whose book *A Journey Through the Secret State of San Francisco* (later *A Generation of Sociopaths*) grew partly out of his time at the fund.
