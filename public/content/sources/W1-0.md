> Charlie Munger's *The Psychology of Human Misjudgment* (Harvard School, 1995), via Farnam Street.
> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with the author.

Munger's *Psychology of Human Misjudgment* is his survey of the psychological tendencies that cause reasoning errors — business-relevant, example-driven, and designed as a checklist, because Munger believed most disasters come from a small set of predictable patterns interacting.

Notable tendencies from the talk:

1. **Reward and punishment super-response.** Behavior follows incentives with such force that "never ask a barber if you need a haircut"; design systems by working backward from what the incentives reward.
2. **Liking / loving tendency and disliking / hating tendency.** We distort facts to favor loved ones/things and ignore virtue in those we hate — including companies and ideas.
3. **Doubt-avoidance tendency.** The brain resolves uncertainty quickly; the habit of suspending judgment (Munger's "I never allow myself to have an opinion on anything that I don't know the other side's argument better than they do") must be trained.
4. **Inconsistency-avoidance tendency.** Commitment to prior conclusions makes people defend sunk positions — avoid declaring commitments that constrain future honesty.
5. **Social proof.** Uncertainty triggers imitation; mass madness (cults, bubbles, safety collapse) is social proof under stress.
6. **Reciprocation.** Concessions and gifts create obligation; used constantly in sales and negotiation.
7. **Authority influence.** Milgram's obedience; check the authority's incentives.
8. **Deprival super-reaction (loss aversion).** Taking something away triggers outsized, irrational defense; used by sales (the "last one") and dangerous in negotiations.
9. **Contrast-caused distortion.** Perception is relative (the real-estate decoy, sequential pricing).
10. **Availability mis-weighing.** Vivid examples dominate statistical reality.
11. **Use-it-or-lose-it and the Lollapalooza effect.** Skills and models decay without practice; the biggest errors occur when several tendencies act in the same direction — the checklists should specifically anticipate aligned bias in high-stakes settings (Munger's example: auction dynamics combine commitment, social proof, deprival, contrast).

Munger's prescription is organizational: structure environments so people are forced toward truth (incentives, checklists, speaking culture, pre-mortems) because willpower alone is not reliable against hardwired tendencies.
