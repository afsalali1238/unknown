> Sequoia Capital presentation to founders, October 2008.
> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with Sequoia Capital.

"RIP Good Times" was Sequoia Capital's October 2008 slide deck — presented by Michael Moritz and partners to its portfolio CEOs weeks after Lehman Brothers collapsed — ordering companies to prepare for a severe, prolonged downturn. It became the canonical recession-survival playbook in tech, re-presented and updated in later crises ("Adapting to Endure" in 2022; Y Combinator's "Economic Downturn" memo of the same period).

The core message: the cheap-money boom was over, capital markets were closing, and companies had to move from growth-at-any-cost to survival mode. The deck's famous opening image was a gravestone for "Good Times," and the recurring thesis: **the companies that survive are the ones that cut early and cut deep, before they have to**.

The playbook elements:

- **Cash is king and runway is strategy.** Know your "the number" — dollars in the bank, monthly burn, and months of runway. Extend runway to multi-year horizons; assume funding rounds will not happen for years and will value companies off survival rather than growth.
- **Cut to profitability fast.** Reduce headcount, marketing, and experiments decisively; don't die of a thousand cuts with repeated small layoffs — one real reduction preserves morale and cash.
- **Growth metrics get re-priced.** Growth funded by burning other people's money stops being rewarded; unit economics, payback periods, and actual margin become the basis of valuation. Customers also pull in spending, so revenue pipelines should be stress-tested for churn and deferred deals.
- **Opportunity within crisis.** Downturns kill weak competitors, make talent available, reduce ad and real-estate costs, and let disciplined companies take share; the companies founded or retooled in hardship (Airbnb, Stripe, and earlier Google/Facebook in softer patches) often dominate the next decade.
- **Psychological reset.** The deck warned founders against denial — expecting the downturn to be brief like the month-long 2001 dip — and framed frugality as a discipline, not a punishment.

The historical significance: Sequoia's position in the venture ecosystem gave the deck outsized influence; it spread beyond its portfolio and set the tone for how Silicon Valley responds to credit contraction. Critics note the advice also accelerates downturns (pro-cyclical layoff advice across portfolios amplifies layoffs) and benefits cash-rich incumbents and top funds over everyone else. The durable operational content applies to any organization facing environment shock: stress-test the cash runway before you need to, distinguish survival spending from growth spending, cut once and early, and treat downturns as redistribution points where market share moves to the companies that kept building.
