> House-curated overview of multiverse cosmology.
> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with the cited authors.

Our universe merges and feeds. Galaxies collide and merge — the Milky Way will merge with Andromeda in ~4.5 billion years; small galaxies are being absorbed by larger ones right now ("galactic cannibalism" is the standard technical term). Black holes merge and sink to cluster centers. On cosmic scales, speculation extends to universes merging and reproducing — partly serious cosmology, partly speculative beyond testability.

Several distinct multiverse frameworks appear in physics, each with different status:

1. **Eternal inflation bubble universes (Guth, Linde, Vilenkin).** Once inflation starts, quantum fluctuations ensure it ends in some regions but continues forever in others — an inflating sea producing pocket universes like bubbles. Our Big Bang is the end of inflation in our bubble; other bubbles may have different physical constants. Bubble collisions are in principle searchable (scars in the cosmic microwave background); surveys have found no convincing signal.
2. **The string-theory landscape.** Compactifying extra dimensions yields ~10^500 possible vacuum configurations, each with different constants — combined with eternal inflation, this gives a population of universes in which anthropic selection (observers only arise in life-compatible bubbles) is sometimes invoked to explain fine-tuning. Controversial as science, because non-testable configurations proliferate.
3. **Many-worlds (Everett).** Not other *places* but other branches of the wavefunction — quantum superposition as parallel non-interacting histories.
4. **Cyclic/merging universes.** Conformal cyclic cosmology (Penrose) posits that the cold, dilute end state of one expansion aeon maps mathematically to the hot big bang of the next (with disputed empirical claims of CMB anomalies). Lee Smolin's cosmological natural selection is the boldest "baby universes" idea: black holes may seed new universes on the other side, with inheritable mutations in their constants — producing cosmic evolution in which universes with many black holes (and thus, incidentally, the physics that permits stars and life) out-reproduce others. It is speculative but makes predictions (e.g., upper bounds on neutron-star mass, argued to be testable).

The throughline is selection logic — Darwinism as an abstract pattern: wherever you have populations, variation, heritability, and differential survival/reproduction, design-like fit emerges without a designer. Galaxies, black holes, cosmological models, and ideas all exhibit differential propagation. Two cautions dominate the scientific discourse. First, testability: multiverse theories that make no predictions accessible from our bubble are arguably not empirical science yet — "multiverse fatigue" even among cosmologists for unfalsifiable variants. Second, the anthropic reasoning is subtle: observer selection effects (we must observe a universe compatible with observers) are legitimate but easy to misuse — they predict observers, not every feature of our universe.

For non-physicists the durable takeaways: the line between physical cosmology and mathematical possibility is actively negotiated; "consistent with equations" is necessary, not sufficient; even speculative cosmology trains the right reflex — checking whether an explanation could be an observer-selection artifact — which transfers to probability puzzles, survivorship bias in markets and careers (we observe winners; the merging/collapsed "baby businesses" are invisible), and history written by surviving universes of every kind.
