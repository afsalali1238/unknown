Dieter Rams (born 1932) is a German industrial designer most closely associated with the consumer products company Braun and the furniture company Vitsœ, and with the functionalist school of industrial design. His unobtrusive approach and his belief in "less, but better" (*Weniger, aber besser*) influenced design practice, twentieth-century aesthetics, and — famously — the design language of Apple, whose chief designer Jonathan Ive described Rams's work as a major inspiration. "Indifference towards people and the reality in which they live," Rams said, "is actually the one and only cardinal sin in design."

Rams joined Braun in 1955 and became head of design in 1961, a position he held until retiring in 1997. Working in the lineage of the Ulm School of Design and modernism, he and his colleagues produced austere, user-friendly products — the SK 4 radiogram ("Snow White's coffin"), which broke appliances free from furniture-styling; calculators, shavers, record players, and the cylindrical T2 lighter. At Vitsœ he designed the 606 Universal Shelving System, a modular shelving program that has remained in production since 1960.

Rams distilled his philosophy into ten principles — good design is:

1. **Innovative** — design should always develop alongside technology; innovation can never be an end in itself.
2. **Makes a product useful** — a product is bought to be used; it has to satisfy functional, psychological, and aesthetic criteria, and anything that does not contribute to usefulness detracts.
3. **Aesthetic** — the aesthetic quality of a product is integral to its usefulness, because products used every day affect people and their well-being.
4. **Makes a product understandable** — design should clarify the product's structure and, better, make the product self-explanatory, expressing its function.
5. **Unobtrusive** — products are tools, not decorations or works of art; they should be neutral and restrained, leaving room for the user's self-expression.
6. **Honest** — design should not make a product seem more innovative, powerful, or valuable than it really is; it must not manipulate the consumer with promises it cannot keep.
7. **Long-lasting** — avoiding the fashionable and thus never seeming antiquated; good design lasts, in contrast to the throwaway culture of fashion-driven consumer goods.
8. **Thorough down to the last detail** — nothing must be arbitrary or left to chance; care and accuracy in detail show respect for the user.
9. **Environmentally friendly** — design makes an important contribution to preserving the environment, conserving resources and minimizing physical and visual pollution.
10. **As little design as possible** — less but better, because concentrating on the essential aspects means products are not burdened with non-essentials.

The principles amount to a moral position: the designer's job is not self-expression but service — to the user's comprehension, to durability, to restraint. Rams's famous "less but better" anticipated the later minimalism of both hardware and software interfaces, and his critique of planned obsolescence and environmental waste predated sustainable design as a mainstream concern.
