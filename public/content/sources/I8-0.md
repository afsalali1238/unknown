> House-curated overview of Mary Meeker's trend reports (Morgan Stanley 1995–2018; Bond 2019–2025, including the 340-page "Trends – AI" 2025 report).
> Archived 2026-09-06 for personal offline reading. All rights remain with the cited authors.

Mary Meeker's annual "Internet Trends" reports (originally at Morgan Stanley, later with Bond Capital) became Silicon Valley's canonical data deck for technology shifts — dense, chart-forward compendiums of the global state of digital markets, read by executives and investors each year. The series began in 1995 with "The Internet Report," which predicted web growth from a tiny base; the 2010s editions documented smartphone saturation, mobile commerce in China (WeChat super-app, livestreaming, Alibaba logistics), the rise of ads-supported platforms, on-demand labor, and SaaS economics.

The 2019 Bond report documented deceleration: smartphone unit shipments and internet-user growth were flattening (~6%/yr), ad spending migrating to mobile was already largely complete, and the industry moved from acquisition-driven expansion to usage-time competition — data the report used to argue the cheap-user-growth era was ending. Subsequent updates tracked COVID-era digitization acceleration, the e-commerce overshoot and correction, and the turn to AI.

The 2025 report, "Trends: Artificial Intelligence" (340 pages, co-authored with Alexander Weiss and Alex Gordon), became the most cited AI landscape data deck of its year. Headline content: AI usage data — ChatGPT reached 600 million weekly users in roughly two years, far faster than the web or mobile adoption curves; roughly every American had tried AI; enterprise adoption concentrated in coding, customer support, content, and analysis, with consumer subscription willingness-to-pay proven (~$20/month tiers). Compute economics: training-cost curves rising into the hundreds of millions for frontier models, offset by inference cost declines (cost per token falling ~10x per year, enabling new products). The report documented AI infrastructure buildout at unprecedented speed (~$300 billion annualized data-center spend), the energy bottleneck, revenue ramp of leading labs from near-zero to run-rate billions, disruption risk in white-collar services (legal, search, media, software), and the competitive landscape of foundation models vs. applications.

Why the reports matter as a genre: they function as the industry's base-rate document — investors cite growth curves and penetration charts in board meetings; founders use the metrics for fundraising narratives; their data-heavy, argument-light style (hundreds of charts) trains readers to reason from adoption curves. Criticisms: trend reports are backward-looking momentum documents that call turns poorly (the 2009 and 2019 editions missed the shape of the next waves); and "Meeker charts" can anchor optimism, lending data-sophistication prestige to consensus calls. The 2025 report, with its AI focus, doubled as a state-of-the-industry brief for policymakers and CEOs — read alongside skeptical analyses (cost sustainability, energy, enterprise stickiness, concentration) for balance.
