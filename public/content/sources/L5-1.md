**Opportunity cost** is the value of the best alternative forgone in making a choice. It is the unvisible line in every decision — not just what a choice costs in money or time, but what the same resources would produce elsewhere. Frederic Bastiat's "That Which Is Seen and That Which Is Unseen" is the canonical statement: the good economist accounts for both the visible effect and the alternative use of the resources.

Every allocation has opportunity cost: money spent on a car cannot be invested; an hour in one meeting cannot be used for deep work; an employee assigned to project A is not available for project B. The cost is rarely zero-priced, which is why it's missed — accounting systems record direct costs, not forgone alternatives. Bad decisions concentrate here: a "profitable" project judged without comparing the next-best use of capital; busyness mistaken for productivity because the invisible alternative (focus, rest, speed) doesn't appear on a timesheet.

Decision applications: (1) compare not against zero but against the best alternative — "is this better than what else I would do with the money, time, talent?"; (2) the baseline of investing is the return of an index, of a Saturday is your best possible Saturday — an outcome that beats nothing but loses to the baseline is a loss; (3) time has rising opportunity cost with wealth/seniority — delegating a $20 task an hour for a person whose hour is worth $200 isn't theory, it's arithmetic; (4) for investments, the discount rate is an opportunity cost concept; for life, saying yes to one thing is saying no to a portfolio of others.

Practical hygiene: explicitly ask "compared to what?" on every spending and calendar decision; keep a "stop doing" list; treat free choices (open source, free content, meetings that cost "nothing") as full-cost, since they spend attention — the least expandable resource; and use pre-commitment (Warren Buffett's 20-slot punch card: imagine you can only make 20 investments in your life) to force decisions through the opportunity-cost lens.
