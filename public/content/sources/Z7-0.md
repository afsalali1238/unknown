Proteins are the building blocks of life, underpinning every biological process; and a protein's function is determined overwhelmingly by its 3D shape — how its chain of amino acids folds. Determining that structure experimentally (X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, NMR) took months to years per protein and had defeated researchers for decades. Christian Anfinsen's 1972 Nobel work showed the sequence *determines* the structure, yet predicting it computationally — the protein folding problem — remained a 50-year grand challenge, with community-wide CASP competitions measuring progress.

AlphaFold, DeepMind's AI system, solves the structure-prediction version of that problem: given a 1D amino-acid sequence, it predicts the 3D atomic structure. AlphaFold 2 (Jumper et al., *Nature* 2021, "Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold") achieved median backbone accuracy competitive with experiment in CASP14 (2020) — a result the field judged a solution to the grand challenge. In July 2021 DeepMind open-sourced the code and, with EMBL's European Bioinformatics Institute, launched the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, free to all.

The July 2022 announcement expanded the database by over 200x: from ~1 million to **over 200 million predicted structures** — nearly all catalogued proteins known to science, spanning plants, bacteria, animals, and fungi, so that most UniProt entries now come with a predicted structure. Within the first year, more than 500,000 researchers from 190 countries accessed the database; the two *Nature* papers drew thousands of citations; structures were integrated into Ensembl, UniProt, and OpenTargets as everyday tooling. Eric Topol: "Determining the 3D structure of a protein used to take many months or years, it now takes seconds... AlphaFold is the singular and momentous advance in life science that demonstrates the power of AI."

The downstream science illustrated the shift: AlphaFold structures helped crack the nuclear pore complex, advanced work on neglected-disease drug targets (leishmaniasis, Chagas disease) with the Drugs for Neglected Diseases initiative, antibiotic discovery, plastic-eating enzymes, and vaccine and therapeutic design. DeepMind consulted 30+ experts across biology, security, and ethics before release.

The model-level lesson: AlphaFold was the first large-scale proof point of AI accelerating scientific discovery directly — not optimizing an existing engineering process but resolving a scientific bottleneck. It also showed the value pattern for high-impact AI in science: a precisely defined predictive problem with a rigorous benchmark (CASP), domain-specific architecture (attention/transformer-based geometry over evolutionary and physical constraints), open release of code and predictions, and databases rather than just papers as the artifact. The remaining frontier — protein *dynamics*, complexes, drug binding, and designing novel proteins rather than predicting natural ones — became the next wave (with AlphaFold 3 extending to ligands, nucleic acids, and complexes in 2024).
